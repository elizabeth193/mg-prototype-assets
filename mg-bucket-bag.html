THE BUCKET BAG

In 2013, Rachel Mansur and Floriana Gavriel introduced a bag with no logo and no hardware, closed with a drawstring. Natural leather on the outside, a quiet colour within.

It was designed with restraint, and recognised for it. Within a few years it had entered the permanent collection of the Victoria and Albert Museum.

Dreamt up in New York and made with artisans in Italy, it is still made the same way today.

THE MATERIAL

Vegetable-tanned leather is a living material. In the first weeks it holds its structure. Over time it softens, takes shape, and deepens in tone with light and with use.

It marks. This is not a flaw but a record, and it makes each bag particular to the person who carries it.

Made to last, and made to change.

THE INTERIOR

Quiet on the outside, colour within. The contrast lining has been part of the bag from the first season — a detail meant for the person carrying it, not the person looking at it.

THE DETAILS

In The Atelier

The leather arrives in hides, not sheets. A cutter works around the natural marks of the material, which is not uniform and is not meant to be. The body is cut, the drawstring channel set, the bag turned by hand before it is stitched. Nothing is glued that can be sewn.

Shop The Bucket Bag →

On The Drawstring

The bag closes with a length of leather cord drawn through its own body. In 2013, when hardware was how a bag announced its value, leaving it off was the point. The shape was left to speak for itself.

Shop The Mini →

Living With Leather

No two bags age alike. A hand, an afternoon of sun, the strap of a coat — each leaves its trace, and the leather keeps it. What looks like wear on another material reads here as a kind of memory.
